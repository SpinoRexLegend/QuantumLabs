From choices to quantum strategies

A game is a situation where your reward depends on what you and someone else do. Here, Alice and Bob each have a qubit: a tiny quantum system that gives either 0 or 1 when measured. We’ll first play with ordinary choices, then add quantum gates and see what changes.

The rules and the vocabulary

Cooperate (C): work together. Defect (D): pursue your own advantage. Rewards are points, not money. Both cooperating gives a good shared reward, but a player can gain more by defecting while the other cooperates. If both defect, both do worse.

Gate: an instruction that changes a qubit. Superposition: a quantum state with amplitudes for more than one possible result. Entanglement: a joint state that cannot be written as separate states for each qubit. Phase: the direction/sign of an amplitude; it can change interference without immediately changing probabilities. Measurement: reading a 0 or 1 with probabilities set by the state.

This lab uses a simple Bell-pair game with Prisoner’s Dilemma rewards. Quantum game theory studies how quantum states and allowed operations change strategic choices. Different preparation gates, strategy sets, and decoding rules define different games; these examples do not establish a universally best strategy.

Your strategy controls

In classical mode, I leaves 0 unchanged and X flips it to 1. In quantum mode, these gates act on an entangled state before decoding, so a gate name is not a promise about the final choice. Change a control to rebuild the lesson circuit, or edit individual gates below.
